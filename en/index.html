Areas of practice

I advise companies that negotiate, buy, sell and supply — where the contract has to hold up in practice.

01

Commercial negotiations

Preparing and conducting negotiations with suppliers, customers and partners: understanding what the other side really wants, strategy, key positions, risk assessment and drafting of the final contract — from the perspective of both supplier and manufacturer.

02

Procurement and supply chain contracts

General terms of purchase, framework and supply agreements, quality agreements. Liability, warranties, contractual penalties, limitations of liability, intellectual property and continuity of supply.

03

Suppliers and retail chains

Commercial terms, rebates and bonuses (including those applied retroactively), penalties and set-offs, abuse of contractual advantage in the trade of agricultural and food products, and related disputes, including court proceedings.

04

Compliance and supply chain due diligence

Policies and codes of conduct, internal procedures, training, counterparty due diligence and whistleblowing systems. Implementation of supply chain due diligence requirements, including the German Supply Chain Due Diligence Act (LkSG) — also for Polish suppliers of German companies.

05

Company law

Incorporating companies from scratch, including filings with the National Court Register (KRS) through the Court Registers Portal (PRS), and ongoing corporate support: appointing and removing management board members and commercial proxies (prokura), resolutions, discharge of board members (absolutorium), approval of financial statements, documents for general meetings, shareholder agreements and transaction support.

06

Civil law and disputes

Contractual and damages claims, debt recovery, settlement negotiations and representation before courts and authorities in Poland.

Approach

Four things that define how I work.

Both sides of the table

I have negotiated and assessed contracts from the perspective of a supplier, a manufacturer and a retail chain. I know which provisions really matter to each party — and where it is worth holding the line.

Three languages, no interpreter

Negotiations, correspondence and documentation in Polish, English and German. A contract does not lose its meaning where legal systems and business cultures meet.

Law that supports the business

I assess legal risks together with business risks and propose solutions that can actually be implemented. I do not block the business — I help it operate within the limits of the law.

On time

I keep deadlines and respond as quickly as I can, because business decisions rarely can wait.

In a negotiation, first find out what the other side really wants. Only then start negotiating.

Professional qualification

I am an attorney-at-law (radca prawny) admitted in Poland. In Poland this is a regulated profession of public trust, governed by the Act of 6 July 1982 on Legal Counsels (ustawa o radcach prawnych) and the Code of Ethics of Legal Counsels (Kodeks Etyki Radcy Prawnego). I am entered on the list of attorneys-at-law (radcowie prawni) and in active practice.

An attorney-at-law (radca prawny) is authorised to give legal advice and opinions, prepare contracts and other legal documents, and represent clients before courts and authorities in Poland. Professional secrecy applies. The title was conferred in Poland; admission relates to Poland and does not confer admission in other jurisdictions.
